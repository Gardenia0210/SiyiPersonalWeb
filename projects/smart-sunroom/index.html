A personalized indoor sunlight-therapy device that reconstructs the beneficial spectral bands of natural light — UVB, UVA, visible and near-infrared — and steers them onto the right part of the body for each user, so people who lack safe sun exposure can still get its physiological benefits.

01 · BACKGROUND

Everyone needs sun — but few get the right dose.

Modern indoor lifestyles cut people off from natural light, yet different groups need sunlight for very different reasons — and face very different risks. Mapping who needs what is the starting point for a personalized device.

Elderly

BENEFIT

Vitamin D synthesis, bone density, circadian & sleep regulation.

RISK

Limited outdoor time, thinning skin — needs gentle, controlled UVB.

Middle-aged

BENEFIT

Mood & sleep stability, cardiovascular and metabolic support.

RISK

Long indoor work hours; sporadic overexposure damages skin.

Young · relaxing

BENEFIT

Serotonin boost, stress relief, better focus and energy.

RISK

Sun-avoidant lifestyle leads to chronic light deficiency.

Young · tanning

BENEFIT

Cosmetic tanning and the mood lift of being outdoors.

RISK

High melanoma risk from uncontrolled UV overexposure.

Children

BENEFIT

Myopia prevention, healthy growth, stronger immunity.

RISK

Sunburn-sensitive skin — needs a carefully limited dose.

02 · RESEARCH · DESIGN VISION

Break sunlight into four controllable bands.

Natural sunlight is a mix of spectral bands, each with a distinct biological job. Instead of copying sunlight as one blunt beam, Smart Sunroom reconstructs it as four independently-controllable bands — so dose can be tuned per user and per body part.

UVB

280–315 nm

Triggers Vitamin D synthesis in the skin — essential for bone and immune health. Potent but must be tightly dosed.

UVA

315–400 nm

Releases nitric oxide, lowering blood pressure and lifting mood. Drives tanning and circadian signalling.

VIS

400–700 nm

Bright visible light entrains the circadian clock, boosting daytime alertness and stabilising sleep.

NIR

700–1400 nm

Penetrates deep into tissue, supporting mitochondrial energy, circulation and cellular repair.

PERSONALIZED DOSE MATRIX

Same four bands, a different prescription per user.

Each user group gets its own dose profile across the four bands — high where the benefit is greatest, minimal where the risk outweighs it.

03 · CONCEPT

A room that follows the sun — and reshapes it.

Smart Sunroom is a glass enclosure that senses incoming sunlight, predicts the sun’s angle, mechanically shades the raw beam, and tops up the missing spectral bands with an LED array — delivering each user their exact prescription.

04 · SYSTEM

Three modules turn a glass room into a light instrument.

Dose control is split across three cooperating modules: a mechanical shading layer, a solar-angle model that predicts the raw sun, and a body-aware targeting model that decides where each band should land.

MODULE 01 · SENSING

Sundial-inspired solar-angle model

A physical sundial sensor feeds a CNN that estimates the sun’s altitude and azimuth from time, date and sky state — so the room predicts where direct light will fall before it arrives.

MODULE 02 · TARGETING

Body-aware light targeting

A limb-recognition model locates each user’s head, torso and limbs in real time, letting the emitter array steer the prescribed band onto the right body part.

MODULE 03 · MECHANICS

Servo-driven modular shading

The glass canopy is split into independently-actuated panels. A servo tilts the louvers of each panel to admit, dim, or fully block the incoming beam — the mechanical half of every dose decision.

MODULE 02 · TARGETING

One photo becomes thirty servo angles.

A Python pipeline captures the body, segments it with YOLOv8, maps it onto a 5×6 grid, applies per-user light rules, and dispatches servo angles to two Arduino Mega controllers.

YOLO-Based Human Body Part Segmentation Model

run_segmentation

Using YOLOv8 segmentation, human body parts can be accurately segmented at the pixel level. The model was tested on real human subjects, non-backless dolls, and backless dolls respectively.

Frame Partitioning and Mapping

grid_mapper.py

The captured image is proportionally divided into a 5 × 6 grid.

Each grid cell is assigned a body-region label based on the body part occupying the largest area within that cell, enabling a direct mapping between the detected body regions and the corresponding glass modules.

MODULE 01 · SENSING

Predicting the sun from eight light sensors.

A photoresistor ring feeds a small 1-D CNN that classifies the solar angle along the north–south and east–west axes — so the canopy knows where the sun is without a live camera.

Photoresistor sensor board

Eight photoresistors face E / S / W / N (two each), every pair split by a fixed resistor into Arduino A0. 100 samples per orientation at 0.5 s each, logged to CSV.

Photoresistor Serial Sampler

open_serial, read_channels

Opens the Mega at 115200 baud, waits for USB reset, then sends R and parses eight comma-separated ADC values. 

Dataset Normalization

normalize_dataset.py

Computes per-channel means at a reference pose (default ns/ew≈90°) and writes norm=(raw-ref)/max(ref,1) CSVs plus normalization_meta.json.

Random-Forest Angle Trainer

train.py

Fits StandardScaler + RandomForestClassifier on flattened 8-D features, evaluates train/val/test, and saves artifacts/model.joblib plus classes.json

Offline Single-Vector Predict

predict.py

Loads the joblib pipeline and predicts a class from eight floats (row0 then row1). Prints ns_deg / ew_deg from classes.json

Integrated, the sensor board and model let the room predict solar angles along both axes and pre-tilt panels before the light arrives.

MODULE 03 · MECHANICS

From flat parts to a working canopy.

Every panel was laser-cut, every linkage 3D-printed. Thirty micro-servos and two high-torque servos were wired to Arduino Mega boards, then mounted on an aluminium-extrusion frame.

A working room that reshapes sunlight.

The full-scale prototype: a transparent servo-driven canopy of 30 tilting glass panels, wired to Arduino Mega controllers. A body lies inside while panels rotate to steer UV, visible and near-infrared light onto targeted regions.
